# My desktop configuration

So, I recently bought a new desktop PC because my old laptop was not enough for
what I wanted to do. It may not look like it, but I really like to code and
develop cool applications. Recently I've been toying with the idea of working
with a decision model like Jev or even a classical statistical machine learning
library solution implemented in OpenCV. I also love to play video-games, but
that's quite obvious. I needed to buy something relatively cheap, powerful and
with enough RAM to do pretty much anything. It should also be small and easy to
carry, just in case I need to move in to a different place or just go back to
Spain. I don't really want to deal with a big 7kg box full of fragile
electronics inside it.

Based on these specs, I considered buying a Steam Machine. It's small, light and
good enough for most AAA games. It comes with only 16GB of RAM, but that's
upgradable. I tried signing up for the Steam Machine orders, but turns out I was
part of the unlucky ones that had to wait. I waited for three full months until
I gave up. I needed something now, not in 2027. So, I decided to expend a bit
more in a Framework Desktop. Really small and light, makes literally no sound
and pretty good for gaming. The only bad thing is the RAM in it. I bought the
32GB version. There's no way to increase or decrease the amount of RAM the
Framework Desktop has. The memory comes soldered to the motherboard because of
AI shenanigans. Long story short, I'll be not running a local LLM in my PC
anytime soon, so this is more than enough. I can code and play games. I can move
this thing around without getting a back injury. It's enough.

# My linux configuration

I use NixOs. That's it. The other day I met a guy that uses Arch (btw), and he
told me he loves it. I told him I use NixOs and he told me mine is even more
hardcore than his. Honestly, it is not. NixOs is quite simple honestly. It only
takes time and headaches. The cool thing is that, you don't really need to deal
with a package manager. At least not like we are used to in linux. You have a
configuration file and a hardware configuration file (that you never need to
touch) That's it. You change everything there and you don't need to worry about
breaking your system.

If you are curious, here's is the whole deterministic and reproducible
configuration I use in my PC. Every single app is defined in there. Every
wallpaper, every font size and every color. Please, take a look at my
[my dotfiles](https://github.com/Chemchu/dotfiles)
